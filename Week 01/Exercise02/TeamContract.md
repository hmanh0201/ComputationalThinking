## 2. Team Goals and Expectations
- **Project Goal:** Apply Computational Thinking to formulate an ill-defined scheduling need into a well-scoped computational problem, leveraging task decomposition and constraint solving to mitigate psychological biases (such as the planning fallacy) while optimizing time blocks via deterministic scheduling algorithms.
- **Quality Expectation:** Demonstrate deep analytical rigor across the Four Pillars of Computational Thinking (Decomposition, Pattern Recognition, Abstraction, and Algorithmic Design) rather than over-focusing on model or framework complexity. Target grade: 10
- **Commitment:** All members commit to contributing equally according to their assigned roles and expertise.

## 3. Communication
- **Official Channels:** Messenger (for quick discussions and file sharing) and Google Docs (for documentation).
- **Expected Response Time:** Respond to Messenger messages within 0.5 hours.
- **Instructor Contact:** The Team Leader will be the sole point of contact with the instructor to avoid confusion and miscommunication.

## 4. Meetings
- **Frequency & Time:** Saturdays afternoon and night
- **Meeting Rules:** Arrive on time. Absences must be notified at least 3 days in advance in the Messenger group. Meeting notes must always be taken by the Leader or on a rotating basis.

## 5. Work Process and Standards
- **Task Management:** Use Leader Documentation to track progress.
- **Git Workflow:** Write clear commit messages (e.g., `feat: add priority algorithm`). Code must be reviewed by at least 1 other member (especially within the Builder group) before merging into the main branch.
- **Quality Standards:** The report must be clear, concise, and strictly adhere to the Four Pillars of Computational Thinking across iterative refinement cycles. The MVP implementation must prioritize deterministic logic, constraint satisfaction, and algorithmic correctness over front-end UI design.

## 6. Decision Making
- The team prioritizes making decisions based on consensus.
- If a consensus cannot be reached, a majority vote will be used. In the event of a tie, the Team Leader will make the final decision.

## 7. Conflict Resolution
- **Step 1:** Resolve directly between the involved members through private messaging on Messenger.
- **Step 2:** If unsuccessful, bring the issue to a team meeting for the Team Leader to facilitate and analyze the pros and cons objectively (strictly no personal attacks).
- **Step 3:** If the issue is still deadlocked and significantly affecting the project's progress, seek advice from the Instructor.

## 8. Accountability and Consequences
- **Violations:** Missing internal deadlines, unexcused meeting absences, or clearly unequal contributions.
- **Consequences:**
  - **1st Offense:** Direct warning in the Messenger group.
  - **2nd Offense:** Formal warning and task reassignment.
  - **3rd Offense:** Report to the instructor for individual grade adjustment or removal from the team.

## 9. Academic Integrity & AI Use
- As all members are proficient in AI, utilizing Generative AI (such as Gemini or ChatGPT) for brainstorming, code optimization, and debugging is allowed and encouraged.
- Strictly no blind copy-pasting. AI-generated artifacts are not evidence by themselves; all AI-assisted logic and code must be deeply understood, verified against authoritative technical documentation, and validated through repeatable benchmarks and empirical edge-case testing. External sources must be properly cited.

## 10. Changing the Contract
- The contract can be amended or modified with the agreement of at least 4 members in the team.

## 11. Signatures
- By being included in the submitted report list, all members confirm they have read, understood, and agreed to abide by the terms above.
